# Bias Playbook

One entry per bias in the selector: what it is, a worked before/after, a real site that uses it, how strong the evidence is, and where it turns into abuse. Evidence labels: **strong** (replicated, large), **moderate** (replicated, smaller or context-bound), **weak** (mixed replication; test before relying on it). Real-site examples were checked on 2026-11-23 from homepage text; sites change, so re-fetch before citing one to a user.

## Framing (gain vs. loss)

- **What:** the same fact presented as gain or loss changes the choice.
- **Before / after:** "See why 3,200+ customers love us" -> "Join 3,200+ people who now smile freely". "Package price" -> "Your investment".
- **Real site:** Fairway Lawns: "Join 78,921+ with lush lawns".
- **Evidence:** strong (Tversky & Kahneman 1981).
- **Abuse line:** a frame that changes the facts, such as hiding a fee inside "investment", is misleading, not framing.

## Loss aversion

- **What:** a loss weighs more than an equal gain.
- **Before / after:** "Save on lawn care" -> "Stop wasting money on treatments that don't work".
- **Real site:** Fairway Lawns, same line.
- **Evidence:** strong for risky choices; the "losses weigh twice as much" ratio is debated (Gal & Rucker 2018).
- **Abuse line:** inflated or fear-based losses for health or safety; any scarcity version goes to influence-psychology.

## Labor illusion

- **What:** buyers value an offer more when they see the effort behind it.
- **Before / after:** "Premium reusable bags" -> a section on fabric weight (GSM), print method, stitching, and why each one means the buyer's customers keep the bag longer.
- **Real site:** Influence Hunter: "We reach out to thousands of Influencers so you don't have to".
- **Evidence:** moderate (Buell & Norton 2011, waiting-time interfaces).
- **Abuse line:** fake progress bars or invented process steps.

## Anchoring

- **What:** the first number seen sets the reference for the next.
- **Before / after:** Basic shown first -> Enterprise or the annual total shown first; Pro's per-user price shown next to Basic's.
- **Real site:** Wild Man Drinking bundles: "Save 41% ($50) $70 | $120".
- **Evidence:** strong (Tversky & Kahneman 1974; replicated in the Many Labs studies).
- **Abuse line:** an invented "was" price. In the EU it must be the lowest price of the prior 30 days.

## Decoy (asymmetric dominance)

- **What:** a third option that is clearly worse than the target makes the target look best.
- **Before / after:** two plans -> add a plan close to Pro in price but missing Pro's key feature.
- **Evidence:** weak to moderate. Strong in the original lab work (Huber, Payne & Puto 1982), small or absent with realistic stimuli (Frederick, Lee & Baskin 2014). Always A/B test it.
- **Abuse line:** a decoy nobody can buy, or a "most popular" label that is not true.

## Default effect

- **What:** people keep the preselected option.
- **Before / after:** no plan selected -> the recommended plan preselected, with the reason shown.
- **Evidence:** strong in policy settings (Johnson & Goldstein 2003); moderate overall (Jachimowicz et al. 2019 meta-analysis).
- **Abuse line:** a preselected paid add-on. In the EU, this is not valid consent (Consumer Rights Directive, Art. 22).

## Cognitive fluency and chunking

- **What:** easy-to-process text feels truer and more likable; working memory holds a few chunks.
- **Before / after:** "Train well, live well, belong." -> one fluent benefit sentence. A 12-item feature list -> 3 groups of 3-4.
- **Real site:** Parcil Safety splits products by buyer (Tactical, Industrial, Welding, Woodworking, Homeowners) and adds a 30-second quiz.
- **Evidence:** strong (Reber & Schwarz 1999; Alter & Oppenheimer 2009). "7 plus or minus 2" (Miller 1956) is revised to about 4 chunks (Cowan 2001); group by 3-5.
- **Abuse line:** none specific; over-simplifying a contract term is the risk.

## Zero-risk bias

- **What:** people overvalue removing one risk fully over reducing several risks partly.
- **Before / after:** "Satisfaction matters to us" -> "Not happy with a treatment? We come back for free."
- **Real site:** Fairway Lawns: "100% satisfaction guarantee with free re-service".
- **Evidence:** moderate (Viscusi, Magat & Huber 1987).
- **Abuse line:** a guarantee with hidden conditions.

## Identifiable victim

- **What:** one named person moves people more than a statistic.
- **Before / after:** "Thousands of accidents prevented" -> a named survivor's story with a photo.
- **Real site:** Volvo shows real crash-survivor stories (example from the video).
- **Evidence:** moderate; the meta-analytic effect is small (Lee & Feeley 2016).
- **Abuse line:** a story without the person's consent, or a composite presented as real.

## Von Restorff and picture superiority

- **What:** the one item that differs is remembered; images are remembered better than words.
- **Before / after:** three accent colors on one screen -> one accent, on the primary CTA. A stock photo -> a real customer who looks like the audience.
- **Evidence:** strong for memory (von Restorff 1933; Nelson et al. 1976). The effect on conversion is unmeasured; test it.
- **Abuse line:** a stock or AI image presented as a real customer.

## Serial position and peak-end

- **What:** first and last items are remembered best; an experience is judged by its peak and its end.
- **Before / after:** the page ends on the FAQ -> it ends on the strongest proof and the guarantee. The thank-you page says "Submitted" -> it says what happens next and when.
- **Evidence:** strong for serial position (Murdock 1962); moderate for peak-end (Kahneman et al. 1993).
- **Abuse line:** none specific.
