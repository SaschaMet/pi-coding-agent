# 09 — Lineage

Where the ideas in this skill come from. Useful when you need to cite something, or when a user asks why the skill recommends what it does.

## Primary source

**Writer Science** (writerscience.com) — an English professor who leads a university writing program in Busan, South Korea, teaching academic and professional writing to researchers, scientists and engineers. The visual guide *Anatomy of a Great Essay* is the compact statement of the model: seven levels from the whole essay down to the clause.

Everything structural in this skill traces to that body of work:

## The ghostwriting and voice material

The five voice archetypes, the transcript method, and the named-framework advice come from a separate professional ghostwriting tradition rather than the academic source. They answer a different question — *how do you make a piece sound like a specific person* — and they are the reason this skill treats voice as a mode set at the gate rather than a fixed rule.

## Sources for individual tools

Cited in the references where they appear: Thomas Mann on difficulty, Cormac McCarthy and Dylan Thomas on deliberate error, Mark Forsyth on alliteration, Oscar Wilde on antithesis, Philip Larkin on prolepsis, Frank Zappa on improvisation, Junot Díaz's workshop silence rule, David Abram's *The Spell of the Sensuous* on synesthesia, Walter Benn Michaels via the steal-style method.

## What this skill deliberately does not adopt

- **"Avoid the passive voice."** Replaced by old-to-new. The passive is the tool that makes handoffs work.
- **"Avoid repetition."** Sentence-level advice that destroys passage-level coherence. Topic words and previewed key terms repeat on purpose.
- **"Write at a fifth-grade level."** Treats language as information only. Register is a choice about the reader.
- **"Write for yourself."** Writing for yourself is journaling. Writing is entering a conversation.
- **"Tell them what you're going to tell them."** A rhetorical rule from an era of speeches. In writing it produces meta-discourse.
- **Universal templates and master prompts.** Coherence emerges between writer, reader and text, so no fixed template can deliver it. The Index-Discussion structure tells you where readers look, never what to say.
