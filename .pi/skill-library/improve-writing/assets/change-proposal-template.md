# Change proposal template

Output this at step 4 — **after diagnosing, before rewriting anything.** Then stop.

The purpose is to separate what you may fix on your own authority from what only the author can decide. Get that line wrong in either direction and the skill fails: too timid and you deliver a copy-edit, too bold and you deliver a page that says something the author did not mean.

Keep it short enough to answer in one message. Number the substance items so the author can reply "1 and 3, not 2".

---

## Template

**Mode:** improve / build / draft / review
**Voice:** preserve / establish (which register) / match (whose)
**Reader I am working to:** … *(from the text, or from what you told me)*

### I will fix these without asking — text-level only

Summarise by kind. Do not itemise every line.

- …
- …

None of these change what the piece says.

### I recommend these but will not touch them — your call

For each: what I found · what it costs the reader · what I would do · what it would change about your meaning.

**1. [Short name]**

- **Found:** …
- **Costs the reader:** …
- **Would do:** …
- **Changes your meaning:** … *(say plainly if it does, and how)*

**2. …**

### Questions

At most three. Each with a default so you can just confirm.

1. … *(default: …)*

### Assumptions

Anything I inferred rather than confirmed. Correct me and the rewrite changes.

- …

## Worked example

**Mode:** improve · **Voice:** preserve · **Reader:** the exec team who approved the acquisition budget six weeks ago

### I will fix these without asking — text-level only

- Turn ~14 nominalized actions back into verbs with a named actor ("the implementation of an examination was undertaken" → "we examined").
- Repair 8 broken sentence handoffs so each sentence hands the next something to pick up.
- Move three long introductory clauses to the ends of their sentences.
- Cut 6 instances of meta-discourse ("as discussed above", "this section will").
- Format the run of lines in section 2 as the list it already is.
- Make "activation rate" consistent — the draft alternates it with "activation %" and "AR".

None of these change what the memo says.

### I recommend these but will not touch them — your call

**1. The Point sits in paragraph nine**

- **Found:** a reader skimming the first screen cannot tell what you are arguing. The draft is accidentally Point-last.
- **Costs the reader:** most of this audience reads the first screen and decides.
- **Would do:** move the claim to the end of the introduction.
- **Changes your meaning:** no. Same claim, earlier.

**2. Section 4 (competitor pricing) does not answer the question the memo poses**

- **Found:** interesting, and unconnected to the activation argument.
- **Costs the reader:** it dilutes the case at the point they are deciding.
- **Would do:** cut it, or move it to an appendix.
- **Changes your meaning:** possibly yes — if pricing is part of why you think activation is the constraint, cutting it removes a load-bearing step. I cannot tell from the text.

**3. No recommendation at the end**

- **Found:** the memo establishes a cost and stops.
- **Costs the reader:** an exec who agrees has nothing to approve.
- **Would do:** add a "what I am asking for" close.
- **Changes your meaning:** yes — it commits you to a specific ask. That is yours to write, not mine.

### Questions

1. Is the drop-off number shareable in this document? *(default: I leave "significant" as written and flag it)*
2. Should this read as a decision request or a status update? *(default: decision request, given the audience)*
3. Section 4 — keep, cut, or appendix? *(default: keep as written, untouched)*

### Assumptions

- The audience is the same exec group named in the opening. If it now includes the board, the concession in paragraph two is too casual.
