# Slop patterns

Read in every mode. Each pattern has an English and a German example, the plain fix, and, where the shape has an honest use, a **fine when** line. A pattern that matches a "fine when" case is not slop: leave it.

## The cause

Slop is a sentence written for its sound, with no fact behind it. Fixing the surface does not fix that. Ask of every sentence: which fact does it carry, and could it stand unchanged in someone else's text? No fact, or yes: find the fact or cut the sentence.

## Sentence formulas

| # | Pattern | Slop (EN · DE) | Plain |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Contrast reveal: "not X, but Y", "X, not Y", "It's not X. It's Y.", "not just X, but Y" | "It's not about speed. It's about trust." · „Nicht dein Angebot ist das Problem, sondern deine Website.“ | "Fast tests get run." · „Die Ursache liegt auf deiner Website.“ |
| S2 | Colon reveal: short setup, colon, punchline | "The culprit: images." · „Mein Ergebnis: zu langsam.“ | "The images make up 1.5 of the 1.9 MB." |
| S3 | Slogan or lesson as the last sentence | "And that changes everything." · „Und genau das lässt sich ändern.“ | End on the fact or the next step, or stop. |
| S4 | Stock opener or announcer | "Here's the thing", "Let's dive in", "The truth is", "In today's fast-paced world" · „Die Wahrheit ist“, „Kurz gesagt“, „Stell dir vor“, „Am Ende des Tages“ | Delete it; start with the matter. |
| S5 | A question, then its own answer | "The result? Faster builds." · „Warum? Weil …“ | One statement. |
| S6 | Verbless verdict fragment | "Problem solved. Next step." · „Diagnose gestellt.“ | A full sentence with subject and verb. |
| S7 | Dramatic reframe | "This proves it can be done." · „… zeigt, was passiert, wenn …“ | Say what the thing does, with the number. |

**Fine when:**

- S1: a factual correction of a belief the reader holds, stated as information: "The GDPR bans processing without a legal basis, not analytics." Never as a reveal or slogan.
- S2: labels, lists, and the lead-in to a quote or a code block.
- S5: a real question the reader is asking, answered in the next paragraph rather than in a two-word stub.
- S6: bullet items, table cells, labels, headings, and a pointer like "Docs: example.com".

## Rhythm and shape

| # | Pattern | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| S8 | Reflex triplets: three items where the facts have two or four | List what is there. |
| S9 | Staccato: runs of short sentences of the same length | Mix lengths. Join related facts with "and", "because", or a relative clause. |
| S10 | Forced symmetry and anaphora: "You don't need X. You don't need Y." | Say it once. |
| S11 | The same frame in every list item: bold label, colon, "This matters because …" | Write each item as its own sentence. |
| S12 | A summary or lesson line closing every paragraph | Not every paragraph needs a last word. |

**Fine when:** S8, the text names three real, separate things. S9, a numbered procedure where each step is one action.

## Words

| # | Pattern | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| S13 | Abstract or inflated words instead of the observation: leverage, seamless, robust, unlock, empower, transformative, landscape, delve · Klarheit, Potenzial, Mehrwert, Hebel, nahtlos, ganzheitlich | Name what was seen: the page, the field, the number. |
| S14 | Zombie nouns: "perform an analysis of" · „die Durchführung der Anpassung“ | Actor as subject, action as verb: "analyse", „ich passe an“. |
| S15 | Hedges and intensifiers: very, really, actually, basically, quite, generally, practically, virtually, particularly · sehr, wirklich, durchaus, ziemlich, äußerst | Delete, or give the number. |
| S16 | Meta-discourse: "as we'll see", "it's worth noting that", "it is important to understand" · „im Folgenden“, „wie bereits erwähnt“ | Start with the matter. |
| S17 | A fact stated without its observer, where the text already names one | Keep the writer's framing. Never add an observer ("I found", „ich habe gesehen“) the writer did not state. |

**Fine when:** S13, the word is the exact technical term ("robust statistics", "landscape mode"). S15, the qualifier carries real uncertainty (keep-list below).

## Substance

| # | Pattern | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| S18 | Goals or wishes the reader never stated: "You want more customers" when they only said what they sell | Use what they wrote. Name where a scope comes from. |
| S19 | Speculation about the reader's audience presented as insight | Cut it, or cite the data. |
| S20 | Invented concreteness: anecdotes, quotes, numbers nobody observed | Leave a marked gap for the user's own text. |
| S21 | A general rule from a single observation | State the observation only. |
| S22 | Motivational lines, life wisdom, engagement bait: "What's your take?" · „Was denkst du?“ | Delete. End on the next step or on nothing. |

## Keep-list

These look like filler or negation and are not. Cutting them changes meaning, so a cut is substance-level:

- Quantified uncertainty: "usually", "in most cases", "about four weeks" · „meist“, „in der Regel“, „noch“.
- Refusals and warnings: "We can't promise rankings." · „Garantien gibt es nicht.“
- Legal or technical precision, including the S1 correction case.
- The writer's register: du/Sie, you/one, contractions. Keep it as found.

## The test

Read the text aloud. If a sentence sounds polished, dramatic, quotable, symmetrical, or written for a feed, rewrite it as a plain report of what was seen and what follows. The script flags S1, S2, S4, S5, S13, S15, S16, S6 (German only), and S22 (English only); read for the rest.
