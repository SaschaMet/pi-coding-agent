# Report template

Use after a rewrite. Keep it proportionate: on a short piece, three bullets and the gaps are enough. The author wants to know what changed, why it matters to their reader, what you deliberately left alone, and what they still have to supply.

**No new recommendations here.** Recommendations belong in the step-4 proposal, before the rewrite. If the rewrite surfaced something new, list it as a proposal for a next pass — never as something you already applied.

---

## Template

**Mode:** improve / build / draft — **Voice:** preserve / establish (which register) / match
**Assumptions** (only if nobody was available to answer): …

### The frame I worked to

- **Point:** …
- **Status quo it displaces:** …
- **Cost to the reader:** …
- **Reader:** …

### What changed and why

Three to five items, ordered by leverage. Each names the reader consequence, not the rule. Mark approved substance changes as such.

1. **[What you did]** — [what the reader now experiences]
2. …

### What I left alone on purpose

The substance-level items you did not have approval for, and anything of the author's you chose to protect.

- **[What]** — [why it stayed]

### Gaps only you can fill

The material the piece needs that you do not have. Be specific enough to act on.

- **[Where]** — [what is needed] — [why the piece needs it]

### Rules broken on purpose

Only if you broke one. Name it and give the reader-experience reason.

### Diagnostics

Before → after, on the numbers that moved.

## Worked example

**Mode:** improve — **Voice:** preserve

### The frame I worked to

- **Point:** activation, not acquisition, is what is capping our growth.
- **Status quo it displaces:** "we have a top-of-funnel problem — we need more signups."
- **Cost to the reader:** another quarter of ad spend against a funnel that leaks at step two.
- **Reader:** the exec team, who approved the acquisition budget six weeks ago.

### What changed and why

1. **Moved the Point from paragraph nine to the end of the introduction** — the draft was accidentally Point-last, so a reader skimming the first screen could not tell what you were arguing. Now they can.
2. **Rebuilt the introduction on the six slots** — the old opening explained the funnel before establishing that anything was wrong with it. It now concedes the acquisition case, then breaks it, then names the cost in budget terms.
3. **Made *activation* the topic of the middle section's sentences** — the topic strip ran through *the funnel, usability, the second step, confusing products.* Readers could not tell what the section was about. One character now carries it.
4. **Cut the section on competitor pricing** — interesting, but it does not answer the question the piece poses, so it was diluting the argument.
5. **Replaced "the implementation of an examination of the funnel was undertaken"** with "we examined the funnel" — and eleven similar constructions. The actions are now in the verbs.

### Gaps only you can fill

- **Section 2, the drop-off claim** — you write that drop-off is "significant". A single number would carry this section, and I will not guess one.
- **Section 3, no foreground** — the mechanism is explained but nobody is in the room. Did you watch a user hit step two? What did they do? One observed moment would do more than the whole explanation.
- **The conclusion** — you stop at the diagnosis. What do you want this exec team to *do* on Monday? Without it, the piece raises a cost and offers no way out of it.

### Rules broken on purpose

Kept two passive constructions in section 2. Both put the previous sentence's ending at the front of the next, which is exactly what keeps the passage moving.

### Diagnostics

Handoff breaks 11 → 3. Nominalization hits 34 → 9. Meta-discourse 6 → 0. Skim test now outlines the argument; before, it listed four unrelated observations.
